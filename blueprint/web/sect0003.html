0.3 Proof Decomposition

The end goal is the correctness of ABA.Impl. We establish it via a simulation by ABA.Spec and transport the spec’s correctness. The simulation itself is decomposed recursively along the sub-protocol structure.

For a protocol \(P\) with concrete sub-protocols \(Q_1,\dots ,Q_k\), the step “P.Spec simulates P.Impl” is split into:

  1. (Substitution) \(\mathsf{P.Impl}[\mathsf{Q_1.Spec},\dots ,\mathsf{Q_k.Spec}]\) is simulated by P.Impl, using the child simulations \(Q_i\).Spec simulates \(Q_i\).Impl and compositionality.

  2. (Core) P.Spec simulates the hybrid \(\mathsf{P.Impl}[\mathsf{Q_1.Spec},\dots ,\mathsf{Q_k.Spec}]\).

  3. (Transitivity) Compose (1) and (2).

Leaves of the recursion (no sub-protocol, or only the black-box AVSS.Spec) skip the substitution step.

  • ABA.Correct

ABA.Impl satisfies Validity, Agreement and Almost-Sure Termination.

Proof ▶

According to Theorem 10, ABA.Impl \(\sqsubseteq ^f\) ABA.Spec. Moreover, one can prove that ABA.Spec satisfies all three properties which can all be expressed as trace properties or fair trace distribution properties. Thus, by Theorem 3, ABA.Impl satisfies all three properties.

Proof ▶

Before abstraction, both systems only differ by replacing GBCA.Impl (resp. WCC.Impl) by GBCA.Spec (resp. WCC.Spec). However, ABA.Impl is partial-information so we need to relate its belief PLTS.
